    How to read the learning cards

    Spelling chunks are visual memory aids, not claims about spoken syllable boundaries or word roots. Spoken syllables use approximate sound spellings; capitals mark the main stress. Spoken guides are available for 20 positions; the remaining cards explicitly mark this research as pending. IPA, where shown, gives the cited pronunciation. Computer speech is available for supplied spellings, but its accent and accuracy depend on the installed voice.

    Meanings and histories are concise paraphrases. Each completed card links to the dictionary consulted; origin notes have an extra source when needed. Memory tips, examples, and chunk divisions are teaching aids created for this prototype. Meaning and history notes with root or source forms and spelling chunks are available at 499 positions. Where an origin is unknown, this is stated instead of inventing roots.

    The similar-word section offers selected comparisons across the pasted list. Close meanings and more loosely related meanings have separate labels. Comparisons explain the intended sense and important differences; they are editorial teaching aids, not claims that words can always replace each other. They do not mark any book entry as verified.

    Spelling complexity uses an estimated 1–5 teaching scale, not an official or age-based rating. Estimates consider length, repeated letters, uncommon letter patterns, accents, and selected sound-to-spelling difficulties. They rate the supplied spelling and do not verify it against the book.
